Benchmarking & evaluation

When is progress real?

Systematic comparisons of unsupervised network representations.

The research question

Do new graph embedding methods consistently improve downstream performance when compared under a common evaluation protocol?

The work

Our TMLR paper, “Benchmarking and Analysing Unsupervised Network Representation Learning and the Illusion of Progress,” systematically compares network representation learning methods. It examines how evaluation choices, dataset properties, and baselines affect conclusions about progress.

Research artifacts

The open-source NRL_Benchmark repository accompanies this research. The archive also preserves the earlier 2019 consolidation preprint and the KDD 2021 Deep Learning Day presentation as distinct versions; the KDD presentation appears beneath the TMLR paper.

Why this matters

For me, rigorous evaluation is part of representation learning itself: it helps separate useful modeling ideas from effects of experimental choices. This perspective also motivates my emerging interest in evaluating generalization in language models.
